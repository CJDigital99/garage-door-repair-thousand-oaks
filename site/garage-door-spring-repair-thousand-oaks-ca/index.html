Home / Services / Spring Repair & Replacement

If you heard a loud bang from the garage and now the door won’t budge, or the opener is straining and groaning trying to lift it, you’re almost certainly dealing with a broken spring. It’s the single most common call we get from Thousand Oaks homeowners, and it’s genuinely not a DIY fix: garage door springs are under enormous tension and have seriously injured people who tried to replace them without the right training and tools.

Torsion Springs vs. Extension Springs

Most Thousand Oaks homes use torsion springs mounted horizontally on a shaft above the door opening, which wind and unwind to counterbalance the door’s weight. Older homes and some original lower-cost installations sometimes use extension springs instead, running along the horizontal tracks. We carry both on the truck and will identify which one your door uses, assess why it failed, and explain the difference if you’re curious.

Why Springs Fail in Thousand Oaks Specifically

Garage door springs are rated for a set number of open-close cycles, typically 10,000 to 15,000 depending on quality. Unlike colder climates where a sharp cold snap accelerates and often triggers failure, Thousand Oaks’ mild, consistent Mediterranean climate means springs here tend to fail more purely from accumulated cycle count, which actually makes proactive replacement based on age and usage a more reliable predictor than watching for a seasonal warning sign the way you might in a place with harsh winters. That said, the Conejo Valley’s roughly 280 sunny days a year and periodic Santa Ana wind events do add their own wear factors: UV exposure and heat cycling on exposed hardware, and additional mechanical stress during high-wind conditions when a door works harder against wind pressure. If your spring is more than 7 to 10 years old, or your door sees frequent daily use, it’s genuinely worth having it inspected before it fails.

Signs Your Spring Is About to Fail

A spring rarely fails with zero warning. Watch for a door that seems to be getting heavier to lift manually, an opener working noticeably harder or louder than it used to, visible gaps or rust in the spring coils, or a door that doesn’t hold its position when partially open, since a properly tensioned door should stay in place at any height without the opener’s help.

Our Spring Replacement Process

  • We inspect both springs, even if only one has broken. The second spring is usually close behind on cycle count, and mismatched spring age between the two sides is a common cause of doors that hang crooked or wear unevenly even after a single-side repair.
  • We measure your door’s exact weight and dimensions. This matters even more for Thousand Oaks’ higher share of custom and insulated doors, common throughout the area’s hillside and estate neighborhoods, where an off-the-shelf spring rating often won’t be correct.
  • We replace and properly balance the door. A correctly tensioned door should hold its position at any height without opener assistance. This is the test we use to confirm the job was done right.
  • We test full-cycle operation before we leave. Multiple complete cycles, checking for smooth, even, quiet operation on both sides.

What Spring Replacement Typically Costs

Most single-spring replacements in Thousand Oaks fall within a predictable, moderate price range, with custom or heavier insulated doors requiring higher-rated springs at a somewhat higher cost. We’ll confirm your exact quote after inspection and before starting any work.

Frequently Asked Spring Replacement Questions

If Thousand Oaks doesn’t have harsh winters, why do springs still fail here?

Springs are rated for a set number of cycles regardless of climate, and here that cycle-count wear is the primary driver rather than cold-weather brittleness, which makes age and usage a more reliable warning sign than season.

Does Santa Ana wind actually affect my springs?

Indirectly, yes. A door working harder against sustained wind pressure adds mechanical stress, and UV and heat exposure over Thousand Oaks’ many sunny days can accelerate wear on exposed hardware.

Do I need to replace both springs, or just the broken one?

In most cases we recommend replacing both, since the second spring is usually close behind on wear even if it hasn’t failed yet.

Is it dangerous to try opening the door manually with a broken spring?

Yes. The door becomes extremely heavy and difficult to control safely without the spring’s counterbalance.

How long does spring replacement typically take?

Most single or dual spring replacements are completed within an hour or two once the technician is on-site.
